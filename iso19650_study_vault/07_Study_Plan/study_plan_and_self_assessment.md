# Study plan and self-assessment

Eight modules. Each one assumes the previous modules unless the prerequisites say otherwise. Reading times are for the open sources, not for the paywalled standards. If you have a licensed copy of a standard, read the cited clauses beside the guidance. Do not treat this plan as a substitute for the standard.

Source IDs are defined in each folder’s `sources.md`.

## Module 1 — The series and its currency

**Prerequisites.** None.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- Name the published edition and the catalogue stage of ISO 19650 Parts 1 to 5.
- Explain why Parts 1 to 3 can be both “confirmed” and “to be revised”.
- State what ISO/DIS 19650-2 is catalogued to replace, and why its open abstract is not enough to describe that replacement.
- Say which part is out of scope of this vault and why.
- Distinguish a paywalled standard from open guidance.

**Reading list.**

1. [Index](../00_INDEX_AND_STUDY_ORDER.md), the glance table (20 minutes).
2. P1-S01, abstract and life cycle (20 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/68078.html
3. P2-S01 and P2-S02 (25 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/68080.html and https://www.iso.org/standard/89704.html
4. P3-S01 and P3-S02 (25 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/75109.html and https://www.iso.org/standard/90358.html
5. P4-S01 (15 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/78246.html
6. P5-S01 (15 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/74206.html

**Key terms.** Edition, stage code, systematic review, draft international standard, amendment, national annex.

**Self-check.**

1. What is the publication year of each published part from 1 to 5?
2. Which part was confirmed in 2025, and what stage code does the catalogue show?
3. Which single draft is catalogued as the replacement of both Part 2 and Part 3?
4. Why is it unsafe to describe the future operational process from the ISO/DIS 19650-2 abstract alone?
5. What is ISO 19650-6, and why is it not a module in this plan?
6. What may you copy from an ISO standard into your own notes, according to this vault’s rules?

## Module 2 — Parties and the requirements cascade

**Prerequisites.** Module 1.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- Define appointing party, lead appointed party, appointed party, delivery team, and task team without using “client” as the only meaning.
- Place OIR, AIR, PIR, and EIR in the order the UK guidance describes.
- Explain why there is one PIR per project but an EIR per appointment.
- Give an example in which a lead appointed party adds requirements for its supply chain.
- Say who creates OIR and AIR.

**Reading list.**

1. P1-S03, sections 4 and 5 (70 minutes). https://imiframework.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Information-Management-according-to-BS-EN-ISO-19650_-Guidance-Part-1_Concepts_2ndEdition.pdf
2. P1-S05, sections 1.2 to 1.4 (90 minutes). https://www.ukbimframework.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Guidance-Part-D_Developing-information-requirements_Edition-2.pdf
3. P2-S06, section 2 and Table 3 (30 minutes). https://www.ukbimframework.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Guidance-Part-A_The-information-management-function-and-resources_Edition-2.pdf

**Key terms.** Appointing party, lead appointed party, appointed party, delivery team, task team, OIR, PIR, AIR, EIR, appointment.

**Self-check.**

1. Can an organization be a lead appointed party and also contain a task team?
2. From which requirements are AIR and PIR derived?
3. How many PIR sets does one project have?
4. Which clauses does Guidance D cite for the appointing party’s EIR to a lead appointed party, in delivery and in operation?
5. Why is an inexperienced appointing party warned against an over-detailed EIR?
6. Who leads creation of the AIR when the organization has a facilities team?

**Exercise.** Take one organizational purpose, “report annual energy use of the teaching estate”. Write four lines: one OIR purpose, one AIR item for a single building, one PIR decision this purpose affects on a refurbishment, and one EIR line for the mechanical appointment. Do not invent clause text. Label each line with the resource name.

## Module 3 — Containers, the CDE, and level of information need

**Prerequisites.** Module 2.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- Explain an information container and why a model is a collection of containers.
- Describe work in progress, shared, published, and archive without treating archive as deletion.
- State the metadata ISO 19650-1 clause 12.1 recommends, as cited by Guidance C.
- Define level of information need and its three subdivisions in the 2021 UK guidance.
- Name the 2024 international standard that now carries the concepts, and the withdrawn European predecessor.

**Reading list.**

1. P1-S03, section 6 (50 minutes).
2. P1-S04, sections 1 and 2, and section 5.3 (60 minutes). https://www.ukbimframework.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Guidance-Part-C_Facilitating-the-common-data-environment-workflow-and-technical-solutions_Edition-1.pdf
3. P1-S05, section 1.2.4 (30 minutes).
4. P6-S01 and P6-S02 (30 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/82914.html and https://www.nen.nl/en/nen-en-17412-1-2020-en-277555

**Key terms.** Information container, federation strategy, work in progress, shared, published, archive, revision code, status code, level of information need.

**Self-check.**

1. Why is a federated geometric model not the whole information model?
2. Who can see a container while it is work in progress?
3. What two acts does Guidance C require before information is described as published?
4. What does archive record?
5. Why does the guidance say “level of information need” must not be abbreviated?
6. On what date does NEN record the withdrawal of NEN-EN 17412-1:2020, and what replaced it?

**Exercise.** For a fire door at technical design, write one geometrical need, one alphanumerical need, and one documentation need. State the purpose in one sentence. Then say which of the three an IDS 1.0 file could check, and which it could not, using the facet list in Module 8’s reading if you have already looked ahead, or leave that sentence until Module 8.

## Module 4 — Delivery phase

**Prerequisites.** Modules 2 and 3.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- State what a TIDP must identify for each container, according to Guidance F.
- Say what the information protocol is for, and what the delivery team’s risk register is about.

**Key terms.** Assessment and need, invitation to tender, tender response, appointment, mobilization, collaborative production, information model delivery, project close-out, BEP, TIDP, MIDP, information protocol, risk register.

**Self-check.**

1. Which clauses are repeated for every appointment, and which are not?
2. Who establishes the pre-appointment BIM execution plan?

**Exercise.** Draft one detailed responsibility-matrix row for a structural drawing container. Include container name or title, task team, level of information need in words, predecessor, and delivery milestone relative to a key decision. Then write the matching risk-register line if that predecessor is not yet appointed.

## Module 5 — Operational phase

**Prerequisites.** Modules 2 and 4.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- Describe the operational phase in the words of the open abstract.
- Explain a trigger event and the difference between pathways B, C, and D.
- State what moves between the PIM and the AIM at the start of delivery and at the start of operation.
- Explain why an in-house works team is still inside the process.
- Apply proportionality without dropping a statutory information need.

**Reading list.**

1. P3-S04 (20 minutes). https://committee.iso.org/sites/tc59/home/news/content-left-area/news-and-updates/new-standard-for-more-efficient.html
2. P3-S03, sections 2 to 6 (2.5 hours). https://www.ukbimframework.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Guidance-Part-3_Operational-phase-of-the-asset-life-cycle_Edition-2.pdf
3. P3-S05, sections 1.3 and 1.4 (45 minutes).

**Key terms.** Trigger event, pathway B, pathway C, pathway D, AIM, AIR, EIR (ISO 19650-3), proportionality.

**Self-check.**

1. Who is the appointing party in Part 3?
2. Which pathway procures and mobilizes before the trigger?
3. What is different about acquiring an existing asset?
4. Can information move between PIM and AIM during the delivery phase, or only at the two end points?
5. Why does acceptance of a PIM not automatically complete the AIM?
6. Give one example of a proportionate simplification from Guidance Part 3.

**Exercise.** A school boiler is serviced every year, and a flood damages the plant room without warning. For each event, name the pathway, say whether appointment happens before or after the event, and write one AIR line and one EIR line that the event would update.

## Module 6 — Information exchange

**Prerequisites.** Modules 3 and 4. Module 5 helps but is not required to start.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- State the purpose of ISO 19650-4 from the open abstract.
- Explain “shall consider” without calling it optional.
- Name the six clause 7 criteria and give one failure for each.
- Separate an EIR test from a clause 7 test.
- Describe when a container is shared and when it is published.

**Reading list.**

1. P4-S01 (20 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/78246.html
2. P4-S02, sections 4, 5, and 7 (70 minutes). https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/preview/21112102/ISO_19650_Guidance_4_Information%20exchange_Edition%201.pdf
3. P4-S03, section 2.1 and section 5.3 (30 minutes).

**Key terms.** Information provider, information receiver, information reviewer, decision point, conformance, continuity, communication, consistency, completeness, information sustainability.

**Self-check.**

1. Which earlier parts’ activities does Part 4 apply to?
2. Why can the same company be a provider on one exchange and a receiver on the next?
3. What does clause 7.7 refuse to prescribe?
4. A space name changes between two issues. Which criterion is that an example of?
5. An XML file will not parse. Which criterion is that?
6. Why does Guidance 4 recommend sharing often and publishing once?

**Exercise.** Take a shared architectural model that fails three ways: the file name breaks the project naming rule, a wall overlaps a column in a federated view, and the material property is empty. Name the clause 7 criterion for each failure, and say whether the container should remain work in progress.

## Module 7 — Security-minded approach

**Prerequisites.** Modules 2 and 4.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- State the purpose of ISO 19650-5 from the open abstract, including commercial and personal information.
- Summarise the NPSA process from assessment to audit.
- Distinguish a security strategy from a security management plan.
- Explain how clause 9.2 enters an appointment even though Part 5 does not use the term “information protocol”.
- Refuse to treat PAS 1192-5 as the current specification.

**Reading list.**

1. P5-S01 (15 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/74206.html

**Key terms.** Security-minded approach, sensitivity assessment, security triage, security strategy, security management plan, breach and incident management plan, security information requirements.

**Self-check.**

1. Which PAS does NPSA say ISO 19650-5 replaces?
2. What must be retained even before you know whether clauses 5 to 9 apply?
3. Name three contents of the security management plan from the NPSA introduction.
4. Does Part 5 define an information protocol? What does it require instead?
5. Where should standards identified in the security management plan be considered?
6. Why are the codes ST1 to ST4 not in the glossary of this vault?

**Exercise.** A survey of underground ducts around a hospital energy centre may reveal routes that should not be widely shared. Write a six-line triage note: the asset, why it might be sensitive, whether you would adopt the security-minded approach, what you would retain, one mitigation, and one metadata rule you would put in the information standard. Do not invent a clause number.

## Module 8 — IFC, IDS, and bSDD

**Prerequisites.** Modules 3 and 6.

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this module, you can:

- Identify the current IFC ISO edition and the buildingSMART release that corresponds to it.
- Describe an IDS 1.0 specification as description, applicability, and requirements.
- Name the six facets and notice that geometry is not one of them.
- Explain what bSDD is, and what it is not.
- Reject a sample file whose schema location is still 0.9.7.

**Reading list.**

1. P6-S05 and P6-S06 (30 minutes). https://www.iso.org/standard/84123.html and https://technical.buildingsmart.org/standards/ifc/ifc-schema-specifications/
2. P6-S07 (25 minutes). https://technical.buildingsmart.org/standards/ifc/
3. Local file `buildingsmart_ids-1.0-documentation-readme_2024.md` (20 minutes) and `buildingsmart_ids-1.0-specifications_2024.md` (40 minutes), both from P6-S09.
4. P6-S11 (30 minutes). https://www.buildingsmart.org/users/services/buildingsmart-data-dictionary/
5. P6-S12 (25 minutes). https://technical.buildingsmart.org/services/bsdd/referencing-bsdd-in-ids-and-ifc/

**Key terms.** IFC, model view definition, IDS, applicability, facet, property set, bSDD, URI.

**Self-check.**

1. Which ISO edition did ISO 16739-1:2024 withdraw?
2. What infrastructure domains does the 2024 abstract add?
3. What are the three parts of an IDS specification?
4. List the six facets.
5. Why is the Aachen file in this vault not a 1.0 example?
6. How does an IDS file use bSDD without copying the definition?
7. Does a passed IDS check mean the appointing party has accepted the container?

**Exercise.** In plain English, write one IDS-style specification for spaces in an architectural model: applicability, the property set and property you require, and a complex restriction on the value. State the IFC class in words. Do not claim the sentence is valid XML.
